Custom made Commodore 1702 monitor

I still own my old Amiga 500 and C64, but finding a suitable monitor is not easy. I never had an original Commodore 1702, and I always wanted one in my setup.

I love the classic C64 look and the nostalgia around it, but I also learned that 40+ year-old hardware is a ticking time bomb. And honestly, waiting forever for games to load is not always fun either.

That is why I use a Raspberry Pi for emulation. I wanted a custom 1702-style monitor to complete the setup, with HDMI input. I found this design by TonyLandi on Thingiverse, but I wanted to use an old iPad display (with a driver board from AliExpress), so I needed a different fit.

Since my Fusion 360 skills are decent and adapting the original design would take about as much time as starting over, I decided to design it from scratch.

The design goals were:

  1. Minimize the amount of support material needed for 3D printing.
  2. Use separate components for the front cover and button control panel.
  3. Create a front cover that opens and closes like the original 1702. I used two magnets on the left side to keep it closed.
  4. Keep the middle and front sections together using a friction fit. Adding a magnet here would be a nice future improvement.

I painted the front and middle sections with a beige-gray spray paint (Hornbach).
